# Typology of Intra-Quranic Connections

Expanded reference for the seven relationship types used in the connection diagrams. Each entry: definition, classical name, when to use it as an edge label, and a worked example.

## 1. Tafsīr (تفسير) — Direct explanation

**Definition:** A second verse states explicitly the referent or meaning of an ambiguous expression in the anchor.

**When to use this label:** The anchor contains a `mujmal` (summary) or pronoun-like expression whose meaning is not self-evident, and another verse names what it refers to.

**Example:**
- Anchor: al-Fātiḥa 1:7 — `الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ` ("those upon whom You bestowed favor").
- Tafsīr verse: an-Nisāʾ 4:69 — names them as `النَّبِيِّينَ وَالصِّدِّيقِينَ وَالشُّهَدَاءِ وَالصَّالِحِينَ`.
- Edge label: `açıklar (tafsir)`.

**Example 2:**
- Anchor: al-Qadr 97:1 — `إِنَّا أَنزَلْنَاهُ فِي لَيْلَةِ الْقَدْرِ`. What is "it"?
- Tafsīr verse: ad-Dukhān 44:3 — `إِنَّا أَنزَلْنَاهُ فِي لَيْلَةٍ مُّبَارَكَةٍ`, combined with al-Baqarah 2:185 identifying the month and the revelation. The referent is the Quran.

## 2. Takhṣīṣ al-ʿāmm (تخصيص العام) — Specification of a general statement

**Definition:** The anchor uses general language (`ʿāmm`) that on its face includes everything in a category, and another verse narrows the application.

**When to use this label:** The anchor sounds absolute or unrestricted in scope, and a second verse carves out a subset.

**Example:**
- Anchor: al-Baqarah 2:275 — `وَأَحَلَّ اللَّهُ الْبَيْعَ` ("Allah has permitted trade") — sounds unrestricted.
- Takhṣīṣ verse: same verse 2:275 latter half and 2:278–279 on ribā; al-Māʾidah 5:90 on khamr-related transactions. These specify that "trade" excludes ribawī and ḥarām-substrate sales.
- Edge label: `sınırlar (tahsis)`.

## 3. Taqyīd al-muṭlaq (تقييد المطلق) — Qualification of an unrestricted verse

**Definition:** The anchor mentions an act or category without a qualifying condition, and another verse adds the condition.

**When to use this label:** The anchor lacks an attribute that elsewhere is required.

**Example:**
- Anchor: an-Nisāʾ 4:92 — kaffārah for accidental killing is freeing `رَقَبَةٍ` (a slave) — unqualified.
- Taqyīd verse: in the same passage and others, the slave must be `مُّؤْمِنَةٍ` (believing). Classical jurists carry the qualification across to other kaffārāt where the anchor verse leaves it open.
- Edge label: `kayıtlar (takyid)`.

## 4. Bayān al-mujmal (بيان المجمل) — Clarification of a summary

**Definition:** The anchor summarizes a duty or concept without operational detail, and other verses supply the detail.

**When to use this label:** The anchor names *what* but not *how*.

**Example:**
- Anchor: al-Baqarah 2:43 — `وَأَقِيمُوا الصَّلَاةَ`. "Establish prayer" — but how?
- Bayān verses: an-Nisāʾ 4:101–103 on shortening prayer in travel and in fear; al-Isrāʾ 17:78–79 on times; al-Baqarah 2:144 on direction; al-Muʾminūn 23:1–2 on khushūʿ.
- Edge label: `tafsil eder (beyan)`.

This is one of the richest categories — most ritual duties in the Quran are summary statements clarified across multiple passages.

## 5. Naskh / takhfīf (نسخ / تخفيف) — Abrogation or lightening

**Definition:** A later verse modifies, lightens, or replaces the legal force of an earlier one.

**When to use this label:** Use with care. The classical naskh tradition is contested in modern scholarship. State that you are reporting the classical position; do not adjudicate.

**Example:**
- Anchor: al-Baqarah 2:180 — bequest to parents and relatives.
- Modifying verse: classical position holds an-Nisāʾ 4:11–12 (the inheritance shares) restricted or abrogated this for heirs.
- Edge label: `nesheder (nesh)` or `hafifletir (tahfif)` depending on the case.

When using this label, the prose companion must explicitly note that this is the classical position and that there is scholarly disagreement on the scope of naskh in the Quran.

## 6. Naẓīr (نظير) — Parallel

**Definition:** Two verses express the same content in similar form, reinforcing rather than modifying.

**When to use this label:** The verses say substantially the same thing in different places. The connection adds emphasis, context, or a slight angle, but does not change the ruling or referent.

**Example:**
- Anchor: any verse on tawḥīd, e.g., al-Ikhlāṣ 112:1 — `قُلْ هُوَ اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ`.
- Naẓīr verses: aṣ-Ṣāffāt 37:4, al-Anbiyāʾ 21:108, al-Ḥajj 22:34.
- Edge label: `paraleldir (nazir)`.

Use this label when the user wants to feel the Quran's internal coherence — the way the same theological core surfaces across very different surahs.

## 7. Taʿqīb / istidrāk (تعقيب / استدراك) — Qualification by contrast

**Definition:** A verse that adds a caveat, exception, or counterbalance to the apparent thrust of the anchor.

**When to use this label:** The anchor leans hard in one direction (severity, leniency, generality), and another verse pulls back or expands.

**Example:**
- Anchor: al-Mā'idah 5:33 — severe penalty for `الَّذِينَ يُحَارِبُونَ اللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُ`.
- Istidrāk verse: al-Mā'idah 5:34 — `إِلَّا الَّذِينَ تَابُوا مِن قَبْلِ أَن تَقْدِرُوا عَلَيْهِمْ`. Repentance before capture lifts the penalty.
- Edge label: `istisna eder (istidrak)`.

This is often the immediately following verse — a structural feature of Quranic style.

## Choosing between labels

Some connections fit multiple categories. Pick the label that captures the strongest operative relationship:

- If the second verse *names* what the first leaves vague → tafsīr.
- If the second verse *narrows* a category the first stated broadly → takhṣīṣ.
- If the second verse *adds a condition* to an act → taqyīd.
- If the second verse *operationalizes* a duty → bayān.
- If the second verse *changes the ruling* → naskh.
- If the second verse *says the same thing again* → naẓīr.
- If the second verse *qualifies or contrasts* → istidrāk.

When genuinely ambiguous, choose the simpler relationship and explain the nuance in the prose companion.
